Start with the work

Assess the workflow before buying hardware.

One useful task

Bring a description of one job, not the confidential files. We start with sample or made-up data, flag the sensitive parts, and decide what stays manual. Good first jobs: summarizing internal documents or cleaning up messy records.

Size for that task

We look at the job, the model it needs, memory, storage and connection before recommending hardware, and we plan who will maintain it. Sometimes a small local model is enough. If it isn't, we tell you before you buy.

What setup covers

Access, testing and a person in charge.

Set the boundary

We set up accounts and give the AI access only to the files and connections the job needs. We agree who can use it and who can change it. We plan encryption, backups and updates with you.

Test before sensitive use

We test the workflow on approved samples and check its outputs against the source. Human review stays in place before anything reaches a customer. Incorrect answers, incomplete records and tasks outside the agreed scope go back to a person.

Keep it usable

We train your people and agree on support: who checks updates, who fixes problems, and how to stop the system. Any remote support access is agreed, limited and logged.

Choose where your data goes

Local means the model runs on your machine.

Choose where the model runs

An assistant can live on your hardware and still send work to a cloud model. We tell you where each task runs before we connect it. If a job must stay local, it runs on a local model, and we test that it does not quietly fall back to the cloud.

Know the limits

Local models are usually slower and less capable than the top cloud models, and they need more hardware. We show you that trade-off for your job. This is setup and training, not a security audit or compliance certification. More in our local and on-prem AI guide.
