Before you buy

Choose the workflow before the configuration.

Start with what you need

Tell us your Mac mini model, memory and storage if you already own it. If not, wait until we understand the job. We assess the workload and model needs before recommending hardware. A machine that runs an agent is not necessarily sized to run the local model you want.

Pick one first workflow

Start with a bounded task such as drafting an internal summary from approved documents. We agree its inputs, expected output and review point, then test on public or synthetic material. Connecting every account on day one makes it harder to see what is working.

What we configure

Useful access, not access to everything.

Separate accounts and approved connections

We set up separate, limited accounts and agree which folders, tools and services the assistant can reach. You decide what stays disconnected. We distinguish reading from sending or changing records, with human review before customer-facing work leaves the system.

Model choice and logs

We choose the model route with you. Some tasks can run locally; others may use a hosted model if that fits your data boundary. Owning the Mac mini does not make every model call local. We review what logs record, who can see them and what should be retained.

After installation

Know what happens when something changes.

Updates and restarts

We agree update and recovery responsibilities, then check how the assistant behaves after a restart. That includes what starts automatically, what needs you to sign in, and how to tell whether the first workflow is running. A failed connection or update needs a visible recovery path, not an assumption that work completed.

Support and practical training

We scope ongoing support, troubleshooting and configuration review with you. Training uses your actual workflow: checking a draft against its source, recognizing a bad result, stopping the assistant and asking for help. Support scope is agreed before handoff; this page does not promise round-the-clock monitoring.

Own the tool

We sell the hammer and teach you to swing it.

Keep control of the setup

The hardware is yours. The aim is to leave you with your configuration and operating guidance, not make you dependent on us for every change. Model providers and connected services have their own terms and costs. We explain those dependencies; changing tools may still require work. Read our ownership and local control guide.

Get hands-on help

Setup and training are available on-site in Ocala and Marion County, or remotely with an agreed access method. If your main question is what can stay on your own machine, start with private AI setup. For the thinking behind the service, hear PJ on The ClawCast, Episode 13.
