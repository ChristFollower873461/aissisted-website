The book

Powered Buildings

How Real Estate Pros Find, Qualify and Broker Small AI Data Center Deals

PJ Standley, B.S. in Engineering Technology · USAII Certified AI Scientist (CAIS™) and Certified AI Consultant (CAIC™)

The only data center book written for the agent who finds the building. The other data center real estate books are written for investors, lenders, developers and builders. This one is for the licensed commercial or industrial agent who drives past an old plant, a printing site, a cold store or a telephone building and wonders whether it matters. It is not an investing book.

A method that works in any state. A ten-minute desk screen built on five gates: power, permits, structure, cooling and fiber. It covers existing buildings and small projects of roughly 1 to 10 megawatts. Florida is the labelled worked example, with sourced notes on Texas, Georgia, Virginia, Ohio and Arizona.

One deal, start to finish. Chapter 14 follows one made-up printing plant from a line in the tax roll to a signed purchase agreement, one document at a time.

It is just as clear about limits. A nameplate is not available power. A nearby substation does not prove capacity. You do not rate a floor. No hype. No promised deals.
